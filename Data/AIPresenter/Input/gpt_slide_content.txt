=== SLIDE | 1 | Deapth-First Traversal ===
Welcome to our exploration of the Depth-First Traversal algorithm. In this approach, we start by visiting a chosen vertex, let's call it 'v'. From there, we continue to visit each unvisited vertex that's adjacent to 'v', diving deeper into the graph. If there are still vertices left unvisited, we simply restart the traversal from one of those. Imagine a diagram showing a graph with vertices labeled from 'a' to 'h', each connected in a unique pattern. This visual helps us see how the `depthFirstSearch()` algorithm works in practice. === SLIDE | 2 | Death-First Search Algorithm ===
Now, let's delve into the Depth-First Search algorithm, often abbreviated as DFS. Picture this: we begin by marking all vertices white. Then, starting from the root vertex, we paint it gray and explore all its white successors. This recursive process continues until all nodes are visited, turning them black. The magic of DFS lies in its use of a stack, managed seamlessly by Java through recursion. This method efficiently traverses a graph with 'n' vertices and 'm' edges in O(n + m) time.
